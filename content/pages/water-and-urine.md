## Why water matters so much

Stones form when urine is concentrated, so there's more waste in less water. The more your Dalmatian drinks and pees, the more dilute the urine and the less chance crystals have to clump together into stones. Vets often say water is the single most important nutrient for preventing urinary stones.

## Easy ways to get more water in

- **Always have fresh water available.** Put down more than one bowl, including one upstairs and one in the garden.
- **Soak dry food** in warm water for a few minutes before serving.
- **Mix in wet food,** which is mostly water.
- **Take water on walks,** especially in warm weather and after running.
- **Offer ice cubes** as a treat on hot days.
- **Let them out to pee often.** Don't leave your dog for long stretches without a chance to go, because urine that sits in the bladder has more time to form crystals.

Don't add salt to food to make your dog drink more unless your vet recommends it. Avoid meat stock and gravy too, because they're high in purines.

## What healthy urine looks like

Pale yellow, almost clear urine is a good sign. Dark yellow, strong-smelling urine usually means your dog needs to drink more.

## Checking urine pH at home

Urate stones form more easily in **acidic** urine. The Dalmatian Club of America's guidance suggests aiming for urine that's roughly **neutral, around pH 6.5 to 7.** Prescription diets for urate stones are designed to push urine towards the alkaline side.

You can check this at home with urine pH test strips from a pharmacy or online.

1. Catch a little urine in a clean container. A soup ladle works well.
2. Dip a strip and read it against the colour chart.
3. Test at roughly the same time of day each time, and write the result down.
4. Share your log with your vet at check-ups.

One odd reading isn't a cause for concern. It's the trend over several days that matters.

**Don't give supplements to change urine pH,** such as potassium citrate, without talking to your vet first. Pushing urine too far towards alkaline can encourage other types of stone.

## When to involve your vet

Your vet can check how concentrated the urine is with a test called **urine specific gravity**. For dogs prone to stones, vets often aim to keep it below about 1.020. If your Dalmatian has had stones before, regular urine checks are well worth it.
