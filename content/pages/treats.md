## Good everyday treats

These are all low in purines (see the [purine guide](/purine-guide)).

- **Cooked egg:** scrambled, boiled or omelette, with no onion or garlic
- **Cheese:** small cubes make great training treats
- **Plain natural yoghurt**
- **Apple slices** with the core and seeds removed
- **Carrot, cucumber or green beans:** raw or lightly cooked
- **Banana, melon, strawberries or blueberries**
- **Plain cooked rice, pasta or potato**
- **Peanut butter:** check the label first, because some brands contain **xylitol**, which is deadly to dogs

## Treats to cut out or keep for rare occasions

Some of the most popular dog treats are among the highest in purines.

- **Liver treats and other organ-based treats:** liver, kidney, heart and lung chews
- **Fish skins and dried sprats, sardines or anchovies**
- **Yeast-based treats and brewer's yeast supplements,** which are often sold for coat health
- **Gravy, meat stock and stock cubes**
- **Game such as venison**

Chicken, turkey, beef and lamb are moderate. They're fine as part of a balanced diet but shouldn't make up most of the treats.

Treats add up. A good rule of thumb is that treats should be no more than about a tenth of what your dog eats each day. If your dog is on a prescription diet, ask your vet before giving any treats at all.

## Never feed these to any dog

These foods are toxic to dogs, Dalmatian or not.

- **Grapes, raisins, sultanas and currants,** including cakes, hot cross buns and mince pies containing them. Even small amounts can cause kidney failure.
- **Chocolate,** especially dark and baking chocolate
- **Xylitol (E967 or birch sugar),** found in sugar-free gum and sweets, some peanut butters and some toothpastes
- **Onions, garlic, leeks and chives,** whether raw, cooked or powdered
- **Macadamia nuts**
- **Alcohol, coffee and caffeine**
- **Raw bread dough**
- **Cooked bones,** which can splinter
