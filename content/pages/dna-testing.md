## The gene behind it

Most dogs turn uric acid into a harmless substance called allantoin. Dalmatians can't do this properly because of a change in a gene called **SLC2A9**, so they pass far more uric acid into their urine. This is called **hyperuricosuria**, often shortened to **HUU**.

It's a recessive condition, so a dog needs two copies of the gene change to be affected. Because of the way the breed developed, **every traditional purebred Dalmatian has two copies.** That's why low-purine feeding matters for the breed.

Not every Dalmatian with high uric acid goes on to form stones. Many never have a problem, and researchers still don't fully understand why some do and others don't.

## What a DNA test tells you

A cheek swab or blood test from your vet shows which versions of the gene your dog has.

- **HU/HU (affected):** high uric acid. This is the usual result for a traditional Dalmatian.
- **N/HU (carrier):** one normal copy, which is enough for normal uric acid levels.
- **N/N (clear):** two normal copies and normal uric acid levels.

The UK Kennel Club has a health programme for this test, so official results can be recorded against a registered dog.

If your Dalmatian comes from traditional lines, the result will almost certainly be HU/HU. The test is most useful for breeders, and for confirming the status of LUA dogs.

## What are LUA Dalmatians?

In 1973, a geneticist and Dalmatian breeder, Dr Robert Schaible, crossed a Dalmatian with a Pointer to bring the normal version of the gene back into the breed. The offspring were then bred back to purebred Dalmatians for many generations. The result is dogs that look and behave like Dalmatians but can carry the normal gene. These are known as **low uric acid (LUA) Dalmatians.**

The UK Kennel Club registered its first LUA Dalmatian in 2010, and the American Kennel Club followed in 2011.

## What this means for you

- **Buying a puppy?** Ask the breeder whether they test for HUU, and whether they breed LUA Dalmatians.
- **Have a traditional Dalmatian?** Assume high uric acid and follow our [three rules](/three-rules): a low-purine diet, plenty of exercise and lots of water.
- **Have an LUA Dalmatian?** A dog with at least one normal copy of the gene isn't expected to have this inherited risk. Good hydration and a sensible diet are still good for any dog, and stones can have other causes, so check with your vet about what's right for your dog.
